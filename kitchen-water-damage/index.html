Kitchen Water Damage Help in West Allis

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. For homeowners in West Allis, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Warning Signs

  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • The ceiling under the kitchen has developed a brown ring
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Flooring Over Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

The Dishwasher Cavity

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. The appliance hides all of it until the floor changes.

The Ice Maker Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.
