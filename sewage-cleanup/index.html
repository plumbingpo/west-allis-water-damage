Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. West Allis Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout West Allis, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
