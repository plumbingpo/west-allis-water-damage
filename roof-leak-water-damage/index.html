Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in West Allis

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. We find the full wet area and dry it out.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. Inside the home, our team takes out saturated insulation, dries the wood and drywall, treats any mold and repairs the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Catch the DripsFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Document What You SeeTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Interior damage from a sudden, accidental roof leak, like one caused by a fallen limb or wind-torn shingles, is often covered under a homeowners policy. A leak put down to an old, worn or neglected roof is usually denied, and fixing the roof may be treated separately from the interior work. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Water backing up at the eave and under the shingles because the gutters are blocked.
  • Rubber boots around plumbing vent pipes that split after years in the sun.
  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Ceiling Drywall

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Wall Cavities

Rain sometimes follows the framing into a wall, where the insulation and the hidden side of the drywall take it up. Bubbling paint or a swollen baseboard may be the only sign.

Attic Insulation

Wet insulation mats down, stops insulating and holds water against the ceiling drywall for days. Drying is sometimes possible with fiberglass. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Roof Framing

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Our Process

How We Handle Roof Leak Water Damage

1

Map Where Water Went

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks wall cavities, ceilings and the attic and charts the whole route the water took, even where the room below shows nothing.

2

Cover and Protect

An exposed roof gets covered with a temporary tarp, or we line things up with your roofing contractor. We also cover floors and contents under the damaged ceiling.

3

Remove and Dry

After removing the insulation and drywall that are past saving, our crew dries the attic and lower rooms with air movers and dehumidifiers and tracks moisture levels until the wood is dry.

4

Treat and Rebuild

Exposed wood gets an antimicrobial treatment where needed. With the roof fixed, our team installs insulation and drywall, finishes the joints, and primes and paints so the repair blends in.
