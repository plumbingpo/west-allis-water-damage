Few problems rattle a homeowner like finding the basement under water. The source may be a burst pipe, a leaking water heater, seepage through the foundation, a sump pump that quit or drains overwhelmed by a heavy rain, and in each case a fast professional response is what limits mold, structural damage and ruined possessions. West Allis Restoration provides 24/7 emergency basement water removal throughout West Allis, using powerful extraction equipment and advanced drying technology to get your basement dry and safe as quickly as possible.

Basements are uniquely hard to dry because they sit below grade, have limited ventilation and tend to hold on to moisture. Moisture gets trapped by the surrounding soil, the poured floor and the block walls, so it will not evaporate naturally. To handle that, our crew uses equipment made for the job, including submersible pumps where the water is deep, truck-mounted units when there is a lot of it, and carefully placed dehumidifiers with air movers built for drying below ground.

Getting the water out is step one, and tracking down why the basement flooded is how we help prevent a repeat. To find contributing factors, we check grading, window wells, the sump pump, penetrations through the walls and the drainage at the foundation. From there you get our recommendations for lasting fixes, which may include waterproofing, interior drainage or installing a backup sump pump to guard the basement against the next intrusion.

Warning Signs for Basement Water Removal

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Efflorescence, a chalky white residue, forming on masonry or concrete
  • Basement air that smells of mold or stale moisture
  • Tile coming loose, carpet stains or floor coverings falling apart downstairs
  • Foundation walls that feel wet, most often near the bottom
  • Corrosion at the bottom of appliances, shelving legs or steel columns
  • Leaks at the window frames below grade, or wells holding water outside them
  • Water seeping through floor cracks, wall joints or around pipe penetrations

Why Basement Water Removal Is Not a DIY Job

A store-bought pump and a few fans cannot do what this job requires, which is specialized gear and trained people. Professional submersible pumps extract water far faster than rental equipment, and our commercial drying systems create the temperature, airflow and humidity conditions needed to dry below-grade structures that naturally resist evaporation. When drying is not done properly, residual water in the framing, the insulation and the concrete ends in mold and material decay.

Basements also present their own safety hazards during a flood. Electrical panels, outlets, appliances and wiring may be submerged or exposed to water, creating a risk of electrocution. The water heater, gas piping and furnace may be damaged too. Our technicians are trained to assess and mitigate these hazards before extraction begins, keeping your family and our crew safe throughout the restoration.

Maintenance Tips for Basement Water Removal

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Use hydraulic cement to close any cracks you can see in the basement floor or walls
  • Clean your gutters so they do not overflow right beside the foundation
  • Covers are worth considering for window wells, which also need to stay free of debris
  • Four times a year, check that the sump pump works by filling the pit with water
  • Put a water alarm near the sump pit and the water heater for early warning
  • Make sure downspouts discharge at least 6 feet away from your foundation
