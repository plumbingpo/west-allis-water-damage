Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in West Allis

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our West Allis crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Heater Closet Drywall

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Attic and Ceilings

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.
