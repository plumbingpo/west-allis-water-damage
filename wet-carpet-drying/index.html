Wet Carpet Drying in West Allis

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For West Allis homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Weeks later, with the spill long forgotten, you get raised seams, squeaks and soft spots.

Mold Under the Carpet

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Water Held in the Pad

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.
