Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in West Allis

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

What It Looks Like

  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Growth Behind the Paint

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Walls You Cannot See

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Water Next Door

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Most walls take 3 to 5 days when professional drying equipment is used. Insulated walls, plaster, vinyl wallpaper and layers of glossy paint slow the process since moisture cannot escape as easily. Without equipment, the inside of a wall may remain damp for weeks, plenty of time for mold to establish.
The bottom of the wall is the place to begin. Watch for a musty smell, a faint line of staining, bubbled or peeling paint and puffy baseboards. The wall may also feel cool or slightly soft. None of these tell you how far the water went, though. That takes a moisture meter, and sometimes a thermal camera, to read behind the surface.
Coverage turns on the cause. When something like a supply hose gives out without warning, policies frequently cover it, wall repairs included. Slow seepage, put off maintenance and flooding from outside generally fall outside regular homeowners coverage. Review your policy or talk with your agent. We can share our moisture maps with your adjuster.
Blending the repair is part of the job, and it usually disappears. The seam gets taped and finished, texture is matched and baseboard goes back or is replaced. What gives a patch away is color, as paint on the wall changes over the years. So the standard approach is painting the full wall, one corner to the other.
Fans help the surface, but they do little for the inside of the wall. Insulation, framing at the floor and the hidden face of the panel hold moisture when nothing circulates behind the surface. A dehumidifier is also needed to pull that moisture out of the room. Should it stay damp or musty beyond one or two days, get a professional inspection.
