Ceiling Water Damage Repair Help in West Allis

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

Warning Signs

  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used
  • Thin cracking in the ceiling finish centered on a wet looking area
  • Drips coming out of a ceiling light, air vent or smoke alarm
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Spreads Above

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

Sagging Sets In

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

Past one week

Sections May Fall

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Damage on the Floor Above

A wet ceiling usually means a wet subfloor or attic deck directly over it. Our inspection includes the space overhead, since moisture left there keeps feeding the problem.

Water Travels Sideways

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

Older Ceiling Materials

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. A bulge can drop a whole section onto whatever is underneath.

Repair or Replace

What Stays and What Goes

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Trace the Leak

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Make It Safe

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Remove and Dry Out

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Rebuild and Paint

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.
