Bathroom Water Damage Help in West Allis

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. We help West Allis homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

A Failed Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

The Room Below

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Slow Vanity Drips

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.
