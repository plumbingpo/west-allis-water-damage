Signs It Is Time for Mold Remediation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A slow leak under a sink, behind an appliance or in the roof that went unnoticed
  • Black, green, white or other colored growth you can see on surfaces in the building
  • Peeling wallpaper, bubbling paint or warped materials that point to hidden moisture
  • Dark staining or discoloration on walls, ceilings or around HVAC vents
  • Cleaning that never lasts, with growth returning to the same surface
  • Recurring respiratory symptoms, allergies or headaches that improve when you are away from home
  • Fuzzy or spotted growth on stored boxes, clothing or the backs of furniture

Why Mold Remediation Is Not a DIY Job

Removing mold is work homeowners should not attempt on their own. Disturbing mold colonies without proper containment sends millions of spores airborne, which can contaminate unaffected areas of your home and create worse health exposure than the original problem. Professional mold remediation uses negative air pressure containment, HEPA-filtered air scrubbers and personal protective equipment so spores are captured and contained during removal.

Professional mold remediation also addresses the root cause of the growth, which is the moisture source. Removing visible mold without correcting the underlying moisture problem means the mold will return, often within weeks. Finding concealed moisture, drying it properly and preventing a repeat all take equipment and training, which certified remediation technicians have.

Maintenance Tips for Mold Remediation

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Give the crawl space proper ventilation, and think about adding a vapor barrier over the soil
  • Clean and maintain your HVAC system and change filters regularly to prevent mold in ductwork
  • Keep indoor humidity below 60% (ideally 30 to 50%) with dehumidifiers and proper ventilation
  • Hang or launder wet fabrics promptly and wipe up spills as they happen
  • In bathrooms, basements and other damp areas, choose paint and drywall rated mold-resistant
  • Overflowing gutters send water into basements and crawl spaces, so clean them on a schedule
