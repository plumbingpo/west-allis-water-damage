Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at West Allis Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate

Structural Drying: What Homeowners Ask

Most of the time there is no need to leave while the structure dries. The equipment is safe but noisy. Ask us about ways to reduce the disruption, for example keeping units away from bedrooms or changing the airflow pattern. Whatever we arrange, the machines still need to run continuously.
For most homes, structural drying runs 3 to 5 days. The schedule depends on how saturated things are, which materials got wet, the ambient conditions and how the building is constructed. When saturation is heavy or the property is commercial, expect 5 to 7 days or beyond. Our technician checks progress every day and updates you on timing.
You will notice the noise from commercial air movers and dehumidifiers. A steady fan running all day is the comparison we hear most. Continuous operation is essential to dry the structure properly, even though we realize it is a nuisance. Turning off equipment extends drying time and increases mold risk.
