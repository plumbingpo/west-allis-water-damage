Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in West Allis

You seldom catch a pipe leak the day it begins. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. Our team in West Allis finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. This is where our team comes in. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the Water SupplyWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Check the Water MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.

Our Leaking Pipe Water Damage Process

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Your agent and the policy itself are the places to confirm. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

  • Whether the damage stayed in one room or spread to others and to the level below.

  • Where the leak is, since an open cabinet costs less than a wall or slab.
  • The amount of flooring, cabinetry, insulation or drywall that must come out and be replaced.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • Leaks at a sink drain joint or trap, which release a little water with every use.
  • Shifting soil or abrasion wearing through water lines that run beneath a concrete slab.
  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Wet Insulation

Batt insulation absorbs a leak and keeps that moisture pressed to the studs. Since a closed wall gives it almost no airflow, soaked insulation is normally removed.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Because the board never shrinks back, a long leak often ends with a new cabinet base.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

Brown water rings and blistered paint show up when a line drips behind a wall or over a ceiling. Wet drywall softens and sags over time, and those sections often have to come out.
