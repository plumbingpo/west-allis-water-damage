Toilet Overflow Cleanup Help in West Allis

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

The first thing our West Allis team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. If it is clean, we extract it and dry the area. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the SupplyTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. A stuck valve can be worked around by taking off the tank lid and lifting the float, which stops the fill.
  2. Stop FlushingDo not flush again or run sinks, showers or the washer. A blockage in the drain line means water sent down any fixture can back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Septic systems that are full or drain fields that are waterlogged, which send water back toward the house.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • Bowl water pushed over the rim by a clog in the drain line or the trap.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Bacteria in Building Materials

Carpet, pad, drywall and baseboards all pick up bacteria from bowl or drain water. Anything porous is normally removed, since it cannot be disinfected with confidence.

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

Drywall edges and the vanity base stand in the water and draw it up like a wick. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Lingering Odor

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.

Ceiling Below

In a second story bathroom, water travels down along the drain pipe and collects on top of the ceiling drywall below, which stains, sags and in some cases collapses.

How We Handle Toilet Overflow Cleanup

2

Extraction and Removal

Our crew removes the standing water, takes out any baseboard, drywall or carpet pad that is contaminated, and pulls the toilet if water got below its base and the flange.

Disinfect Surfaces

Floors, wall bases, fixtures and the subfloor are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products. With Category 3 water this step is done before drying equipment is switched on.

Dry and Verify

We set drying equipment in the bathroom and in the room underneath if water reached it. Our crew keeps metering the subfloor and the ceiling until both read normal again.
