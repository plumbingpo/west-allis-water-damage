Whether the water came from a storm, an overflowing river, a backed-up sewer, a failed sump pump or a burst plumbing line, flood damage needs a trained crew right away. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. For flood emergencies anywhere in West Allis, West Allis Restoration is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

Cleanup opens with rapid extraction: truck-mounted units and submersible pumps that pull thousands of gallons an hour out of the building. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. We tear out and properly dispose of what cannot be saved, such as soaked carpet padding, contaminated drywall and ruined insulation.

Once the space is extracted and sanitized, our team brings in commercial drying gear, namely low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers plus high-velocity air movers, so the structural components dry all the way through. Our crew returns daily, using thermal imaging and calibrated meters to track moisture until the structure is completely dry and mold growth is prevented. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

Signs You Need Flood Cleanup in West Allis

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Receding water that leaves muddy residue and debris behind
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Cracked foundation walls, low doorways or window wells letting water inside
  • Soft, wet drywall or carpeting that is soaked through
  • Water flooding out of a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine
  • A water line on walls or baseboards showing how high it rose

Why Professional Flood Cleanup Matters

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Have a backwater valve fitted to the sewer line so sewage cannot flow back inside
  • Store valuables and important papers off the basement floor in waterproof containers
  • Install a battery backup sump pump so you stay protected when storms knock out power
  • Roof runoff should leave the downspout far enough out that it cannot soak the foundation
  • Coat basement walls with waterproofing and seal any cracks in the foundation
  • Wherever flooding is likely, get appliances and electrical equipment up above floor level
